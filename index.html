Miscalibrated Judges: A Psychometric Evaluation of LLMs as Hate Speech Raters

Mikel K. Ngueajio1,2, Rick Battle3, NaheedAnjum Arafat1, Danda B. Rawat1,
1Howard University 2CMU Africa 3VMWARE NLP Lab
Paper arXiv Code Data

The Hate Thermometer Measuring the hate intensity of a text using LLMs

Abstract

We evaluate 5 LLMs as hate speech raters using the Many-Facet Rasch Model (MFRM), revealing systematic leniency bias and a calibration paradox.

Hate speech annotation is costly, inconsistent, and increasingly delegated to Large Language Models (LLMs). Yet whether LLMs function as reliable psychometric raters remains unknown. We present the Hate Thermometer — the first psychometric evaluation of LLMs as hate speech annotators using the Many-Facet Rasch Model (MFRM).

Across five LLMs, 582 human annotators, and 250 comments from the Measuring Hate Speech corpus, we find that all models except GPT-5 exhibit systematic leniency relative to humans. A counterintuitive calibration paradox emerges: lenient LLMs produce higher calibrated hate scores after joint MFRM correction. LLMs systematically underestimate dehumanization while overestimating genocide and violence. Monolithic prompting consistently outperforms modular prompting in rater consistency. These findings establish MFRM as a principled framework for auditing and correcting LLM annotation bias in hate speech detection.

Key Findings

🌡️ Finding 1: The Leniency Paradox

All LLMs except GPT-5 underestimate hate severity. Yet after joint MFRM calibration, lenient models produce higher calibrated hate scores — because MFRM interprets high scores from lenient raters as strong evidence of true hate severity.

👁️ Finding 2: The Dehumanization Blind Spot

LLMs systematically miss subtle dehumanization. Human threshold: δ = 0.485. LLM threshold: δ = 1.160–1.855. LLMs require far more extreme content before flagging dehumanizing rhetoric.

✅ Finding 3: Better Than Human Crowd-Workers

Carefully configured LLMs apply the rating scale MORE consistently than human crowd-workers. Claude (mono) achieves Infit MS = 1.266 vs. human mean of 2.288 — falling within the ideal Rasch measurement range.
